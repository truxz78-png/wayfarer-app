Where to stay in Lisbon: a neighborhood guide

Alfama wins for atmosphere — narrow lanes, fado music drifting from doorways, and the castle overhead — but its hills are steep and the cobblestones are rough on wheeled luggage. Baixa is flatter and more central, walking distance to the river and the main squares, which makes it the easiest first-timer base. Príncipe Real suits a longer stay: leafy, full of independent cafés, and a short tram ride from everything else. Whichever area you pick, compare live hotel prices for Lisbon here before locking in a date — rates swing a lot between weekdays and weekends in this city.

Hotels vs. Airbnb in Tokyo: which one wins

Tokyo hotels are famous for making the most of very little space — expect a smaller room but excellent service, daily housekeeping, and a front desk that can solve almost any problem in seconds. An apartment rental makes more sense if you're staying over five nights, traveling with a group, or want a kitchen and laundry. For short stays near the main train lines, a hotel usually works out similarly priced once you factor in Airbnb service fees. Compare hotel prices for Tokyo here and check the walking distance to the nearest JR line before booking.

Best areas to stay in Bangkok on a budget

Sukhumvit is the safe default — packed with mid-range hotels, connected to the BTS Skytrain, and walkable to food at almost any hour. Silom is quieter during the day and comes alive at night, with easy access to the river ferries for temple-hopping. Avoid booking anywhere without checking its distance to a Skytrain or MRT station first — Bangkok traffic can turn a short trip into an hour-long ordeal. Compare hotel prices for Bangkok here and filter by proximity to the nearest station.

Where to stay in Mexico City for first-timers

Roma Norte and Condesa are the two neighborhoods everyone recommends, and for good reason — tree-lined streets, strong coffee shops, and a short walk to most of the city's best restaurants. Polanco is more upscale and quieter, with easy access to Chapultepec Park. Both areas sit well above the noisier, more traffic-heavy parts of the city. Compare hotel prices for Mexico City here before you commit to a neighborhood.

The best time — and place — to book a stay in Rome

Prices in Rome swing hard by season: April, May, September, and October give you good weather without peak summer rates or crowds. For location, staying near Termini station is convenient for trains but louder at night; the area around Campo de' Fiori or Trastevere trades a bit of convenience for a much better evening atmosphere. Compare hotel prices for Rome here across a few different dates — even shifting your trip by a few days can change the rate significantly.

Trip prep essentials

How to pack light for any trip

One carry-on, one packing cube system, done. Lay out everything you think you need, then remove a third of it — you'll still overpack. Stick to a neutral color palette so every top works with every bottom, and wear your bulkiest shoes and jacket on travel day instead of packing them. Roll, don't fold, and leave a gap for what you'll inevitably buy along the way.

5 things to check before flying international

Passport validity (most countries want 6 months left), visa requirements for your specific nationality, any required vaccinations, your airline's baggage rules for international legs, and whether your phone plan works abroad or you'll need a local eSIM on arrival. A five-minute check now saves a very bad day at the airport.

The real cost of skipping travel insurance

A single ER visit abroad can run into the thousands, and a missed connection or a canceled tour can erase what you saved by skipping coverage. Good travel insurance is usually a small fraction of your total trip cost — it's the one line item that protects everything else you've already paid for.
